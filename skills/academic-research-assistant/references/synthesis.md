# Synthesis: Literature Review + Outline + Citation Map

This is where the user gets the payoff: a real scaffold they can write from.

## Literature review (≤600 words)
Not a list of summaries. A synthesis with three parts:
- **Consensus** — what do the sources agree on?
- **Debate** — where do they diverge or contradict?
- **Gap** — what's missing that the user's paper can address? *(This is the most valuable sentence you produce — it's the user's contribution.)*

Each claim in the lit review must point to a source by short citation + URL.

## Paper outline
Section-by-section. For a typical empirical paper:
- **Abstract** — 2 sentences: question, approach, expected contribution. (Placeholder; user fills after writing.)
- **Introduction** — the question, why it matters, the gap, the thesis.
- **Related work / Literature review** — drawn from your synthesis.
- **Methods** — how the user will answer the question. Suggest options; don't invent data.
- **Discussion** — expected findings, limitations, implications.
- **Conclusion** — restated contribution.

Under each section: **bullet points of what goes in**, not prose. Example:
> **Introduction**
> - Open with the 2023 WHO stat on adolescent screen time [source 2]
> - Name the gap: existing studies measure hours, not content type
> - Thesis: content type predicts wellbeing more than duration

**Adapt section names to the discipline** — review essay, theoretical paper, policy memo, lab report. Ask the user if the field isn't clear.

## Citation map
A table mapping every factual claim the user might make to its backing source:

| Outline claim | Supporting source(s) | URL |
|---|---|---|
| "Screen time rose 40% among teens 2019–2023" | WHO 2023 report | https://… |
| "Prior work focuses on duration" | Chen & Lee 2021; Patel 2022 | … |

Claims with no source → `[needs source]`. **Never invented.**

## What you do NOT write
- Finished body paragraphs. Bullets and fragments only.
- Data or results the user didn't give you. Suggest method; don't fabricate findings.
- A "complete paper." If asked, remind the user this is a research assistant (see `integrity.md`).
