# Multi-touch Sequence Design

One email almost never converts cold. Sequences do. This defines the default cadence and when to deviate.

## The 3-touch default

| Touch | Channel | Day | Ceiling | Job |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Email | 1 | 90 words | Signal → angle → proof → CTA |
| 2 | LinkedIn | 4 | 60 words | **New** angle or a resource |
| 3 | Email | 8 | 50 words | Breakup — permission to ignore |

## Non-negotiable rules
- **Each touch adds new value.** Touch 2 that says "bumping this up" or "just following up" is a wasted touch and lowers reply rate on touch 3. Bring a new angle, a new proof point, or a useful resource.
- **Cadence respects the inbox.** A 3-day then 4-day gap beats daily spam. Cold outreach that arrives daily trains the prospect to ignore you.
- **One CTA per touch.** Stated once, near the end.
- **Shorter over time.** Touch 1 > Touch 2 > Touch 3. Escalating brevity signals respect.
- **Per-touch ceiling is a hard cap.** 90 / 60 / 50 words is a limit, not a target. Escalating brevity describes the shape (T1 > T2 > T3); it does NOT waive the individual ceiling. A 70-word Touch 2 that's "shorter than Touch 1" is still a failure — cut it to ≤60.
- **Multi-thread only on purpose.** Emailing a second person at the account is fine for enterprise, but CC-ing a stranger on touch 1 feels like escalation. Don't.

## When to deviate

### Enterprise / strategic accounts (5–7 touches over ~4 weeks)
- Add a 2nd email touch between the LinkedIn and breakup.
- Multi-thread: contact a peer (with a *different* angle, not the same email). If no peer is named in the input, write to a **role** (e.g. "VP Engineering") with its own angle rather than skipping multi-thread entirely — still don't CC, still don't reuse the primary's email.
- One touch can be a physical or async asset (a Loom, a one-pager) rather than a message.
- Extend gaps to 5–7 days.

### Inbound / warm lead (1 touch, fast)
- Collapse to a single email within hours.
- Lead with the trigger ("you asked about X"), use a **time** CTA, not an asset CTA — warmth earns higher friction.
- No sequence needed.

### High-signal account (Tier-3 quote in hand)
- **Lead on LinkedIn, not email.** When you have a real thing they said, LinkedIn reply rates roughly double vs. cold email — the context (their post) is right there.
- Mirror their tone and vocabulary from the source.

## Channel rules

### Email
- Subject ≤5 words, a fragment of the signal.
- Body: observation → bridge → proof → CTA. Nothing else.
- No images, no logos, no "preview text" tricks on touch 1. Plain text outperforms.

### LinkedIn
- Connection request note ≤50 words, no pitch. Pitch in the first message after they accept.
- Reference the specific post/action, not "I'd like to connect."
- Every sentence needs a subject + verb. Connection notes are short enough that dropped subjects ("Work on…" → "I work on…") read as fragments — proofread before sending.
- Never paste the email into LinkedIn. It's a different medium; write to the thumb.

## Stopping rules
- **Stop immediately** on any reply, even negative. A "no" is data, not an invitation for touch 2.
- **Stop** if the prospect's role changes (new job) — the angle is now stale. Restart research.
- **Don't extend past breakup.** Touch 4+ to a non-responder is harassment, not persistence.

## Measuring it
For the user running the sequence, the only metrics that matter:
- **Reply rate** (target ≥8% cold; ≥15% with Tier-3 signal)
- **Positive reply rate** (target ≥40% of replies)
- **Meetings booked per 100 sends**

If reply rate is below 4%, the problem is upstream — either research (no real signal) or angle (weak bridge). Fix those before sending more.
