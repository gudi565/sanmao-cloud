# Personalization Depth Guide

"Personalization" is the most abused word in sales. This defines the real thing — and the test that catches the fake version.

## Fake personalization (auto-reject)
These add zero signal and make you look automated:
- Dropping in their first name ("Hi Sarah…")
- "Loved your recent post" with no specifics
- Mentioning their company name where any company would fit
- Complimenting their industry ("love what you're doing in fintech")
- "Congrats on the news!" without naming the news

If removing the personalization leaves a grammatical, sensible message — it wasn't real.

## Real personalization
A real personalization is a **bridge**:

> **[One specific fact about them]** → **[One specific thing your offer does about it]**

Both halves must be specific. "You're a CTO → we help CTOs" is not a bridge; it's a demographic.

### The swap test
Swap the prospect's name, company, and industry for a different prospect's. If the message **still works**, it is not personalized. Rewrite until it breaks when swapped.

## The you/your test
Count pronouns:
- First person: **I, we, our, us, me**
- Second person: **you, your**

**you/your must win at least 2:1.** If it doesn't, the message is about you, not them — and cold prospects don't care about you. Rewrite by converting "We do X" into "You get X."

Bad: *"We help SaaS companies improve retention with our AI platform, and we'd love to show you…"*
Good: *"You're losing 40% of users at step 3. You'd see it within a week if you delayed the activation email — want the teardown?"*

## The specificity ladder
For every claim, climb one rung until you can't defend the next:

| Rung | Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | "improve retention" | ❌ vague |
| 1 | "improve retention 15%" | ⚠️ specific, unproven |
| 2 | "cut onboarding drop-off 40% → 12% at [peer]" | ✅ specific + proven |

**Always climb to the top rung you can defend.** If you can't defend rung 1, don't make the claim — offer an asset instead.

## Length discipline
Long cold emails don't get read; they get archived.

| Touch | Word ceiling | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (email) | 90 | Earn the right to be read |
| 2 (LinkedIn) | 60 | Mobile screen, one thumb |
| 3 (breakup) | 50 | Respect the silence |

If you're over, cut the proof point, not the specificity. Specificity is the whole product.

## Common collapse patterns
- **The "three feature" email** — lists what you do instead of what they get. Auto-reject.
- **The humblebrag opener** — "I've helped 200 founders…" Switch to a question about them.
- **The dual CTA** — "Want to hop on a call, or should I send a demo?" Pick one. The prospect won't.
- **The apology close** — "Sorry for the long email." Don't write the long email.
