# Prospect Research Playbook

The whole skill collapses if your "signals" are generic. This defines what counts as a real signal, where to find them, and the minimum research you must do before writing.

## What counts as a signal
A signal is a **specific, verifiable fact** about this prospect that a stranger could not invent. Three tiers, weakest to strongest:

| Tier | Type | Example | Use alone? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Inferred | "Series B fintech → probably cares about compliance" | ❌ never alone |
| 2 | Public event | "Raised $40M Series B on July 3, 2026" | ⚠️ combined only |
| 3 | Stated | They wrote: *"our onboarding drop-off is killing me"* | ✅ quote it |

**Require at least one Tier-3 signal per prospect.** Tier-1 alone is what spam looks like with extra steps.

## Where to mine signals
1. **LinkedIn (last 90 days)** — posts, comments, job changes, shared articles. The richest single source for Tier-3.
2. **Company blog / newsroom / changelog** — launches, leadership hires, new market moves.
3. **Open job postings** — what they're hiring for reveals where they're investing and where it hurts. A posting for "Head of Onboarding" is a Tier-2 signal about onboarding problems.
4. **Conference talks / podcasts / webinars** — transcripts are gold for Tier-3 quotes. Search the prospect's name + "talk" or "podcast."
5. **GitHub / product docs** — for technical buyers; recent shipped work, open issues they comment on.
6. **Funding, acquisition, exec hires** — Crunchbase, press releases, "X joins Y as CTO."

## The 5-minute research floor
Real outreach requires real research. If the user gives you **no source link**, do not write a message. Instead return a **research brief**:

> "I can't personalize without a real signal. Answer any 2 of these and I'll write a message that doesn't read like spam:
> 1. What's one thing {prospect} publicly said or did in the last 90 days?
> 2. What's a problem they've signaled they have?
> 3. What did they just launch, raise, or hire for?
> 4. Who else on their team would care about {your outcome}?
> 5. What's the cost to them of not solving {problem}?"

This brief is itself a deliverable — it forces the user to do the homework that makes outreach work.

## Extracting the signal → outcome bridge
Once you have a Tier-3 signal, the work is connecting it to one outcome of the offer:

- Signal: *"onboarding drop-off is killing me"*
- Their implied metric: activation rate / day-1 retention
- Your outcome: you cut onboarding drop-off at peer companies
- **Bridge (the angle):** "You said onboarding drop-off is killing you — we cut it from 40% to 12% at [peer]. Is step 3 where you're bleeding?"

One signal. One outcome. One bridge. That's the whole message.

## Anti-patterns that are NOT signals
- ❌ "I noticed Acme is growing fast" — everyone noticed
- ❌ "Love your work in the fintech space" — applies to 5,000 people
- ❌ "Congrats on the recent news" (without naming it)
- ❌ Any quote or metric you cannot point to a source for
- ❌ Tier-1 inferences dressed up as Tier-3 ("as a Series B founder, you must be…")

## When research fails
If after genuine effort you find **no Tier-3 signal**, the honest move is the **Resource Give** framework (see `frameworks.md`) — lead with value you created, ask nothing — rather than faking specificity.
